Logistics · 2026 · Nomax

Ecargo

A system for tracking parcels from China to Mongolia.

Deployed · launching soonCustomer, warehouse and admin apps

The problem

People order from Taobao, ship to a warehouse in Erlian, and parcels are trucked to Ulaanbaatar for pickup. Many smaller cargo companies still track parcels in spreadsheets, so customers often have to call to find out where their parcel is.

What I worked on

Four apps on one API. Customers get an address in China, follow each parcel, pay online and show a pickup code. Warehouse staff weigh, photograph, label and load parcels. Admins manage prices, branches, claims and staff.

Interesting problems

  • Parcel status rules. Every scan goes through one check that knows who is scanning and where. I added it after a mistyped scan once moved the wrong parcel.
  • Handling money carefully. Exact tugrik rounding, logged price corrections, verified payment webhooks, and a job that catches payments whose confirmation got lost.
  • An audit log that stays intact. The database only allows adding entries, and personal details are masked.
  • Warehouse hardware. Thermal labels and receipts, and a web app that still works from inside China.
  1. Pre-alert
  2. Intake in Erlian
  3. Truck to UB
  4. Ready at branch
  5. Pay online
  6. Pickup by code

E-commerce · 2025 → now

Aiyda.store

Shopping from Chinese sellers, in tugrik.

LiveMy own projectaiyda.store ↗

The problem

Many people in Mongolia buy from Taobao, Weidian and Xianyu, but those sites are in Chinese, priced in yuan and don't ship here. Buying usually means messaging a middleman and hoping for the best.

What I worked on

My first long-term project. An online store with 8,000+ products from Chinese sellers, priced in tugrik. After a customer pays, a bot places the order on the Chinese site with the size and colour they picked, and follows it until it reaches their cargo company.

Interesting problems

  • An ordering bot. A Playwright service buys the exact item variant on Taobao or Weidian. Anything it can't finish goes to a list for a person to check.
  • Careful stock checks. A product is only removed when several sources agree it's gone. I added this after a provider error once removed 47 products by mistake.
  • Safe payments. Payment webhooks are verified and can only move an order one step forward, so a repeated message does no harm.
  • Search. Fuzzy search across products, sellers and brands, with cached counts to keep pages fast.
  1. Customer pays in ₮
  2. Price checked again
  3. Bot orders in China
  4. Tracking synced
  5. Arrives at their cargo

Media streaming · 2026

KinoUzyo

A streaming platform for a small private group, with Mongolian subtitles made by AI.

Deployed · invite onlyWeb, Android phone and Android TV

The idea

A Netflix-style experience in Mongolian: browse movies, series, anime and K-drama, press play, and watch on a browser, phone or TV without downloading the file first.

What I worked on

Three backend services, a Next.js web app, and Android phone and TV apps. Resume across devices, a play queue, saved quality and subtitle settings, AI translation of English subtitles into Mongolian, and using your phone as a TV remote.

Interesting problems

  • Starting playback early. The video is converted to streamable chunks while it's still arriving, with a fallback if that fails.
  • Mongolian subtitles with AI. English subtitle tracks are extracted and translated, and the Mongolian track shows up in the player once it's ready.
  • Finding the right episode inside large season packs, including anime seasons split into parts.
  • Switching sources mid-episode while the current one keeps playing, then resuming at the same second.

Mobile app · 2026 · Nomax

Nomady

A travel guide for exploring Mongolia on your own.

In developmentiOS, Android and web from one codebase

The problem

Independent travellers often arrive in Mongolia with few plans and information that's hard to trust, like made-up distances, ratings and map pins. In a country this big, that matters.

What I worked on

A map, a discovery feed and sourced route itineraries, an Arrival planner that suggests an outing from your stay, pace and interests, and a private journey recorder that turns your visits into a route story. Everything stays on your phone.

Interesting problems

  • One map on web and phone. Leaflet runs directly on the web, and on phones inside a locked-down WebView, from the same code.
  • Honest content. Research entries are source-cited and never claim prices, safety or exact locations. Missing data is shown as "Not confirmed".
  • Shared types between the app and the backend functions, with a check that they stay in sync.
  • Offline-first release. One setting turns off accounts and the backend for the first launch.
